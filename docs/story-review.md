# Story and training — review draft

**For review, not an implementation brief.** Start with the playable Attic, then aim for five short woodland chapters. Reuse existing puzzles where they fit; no new polished artwork yet.

## Direction agreed so far

- Older Alisha tells her granddaughter about her adventures. The granddaughter calls Fiona **Aunt Fiona**. Fiona is absent from the attic; we have not explained why.
- The Attic opens the story: tidy photos, assemble a book, craft the Picture Book, then tap it open.
- First meeting: Alisha is playing disc golf in the forest. A wide throw lands in Fiona’s antlers; Alisha follows it and meets her. Keep it funny and harmless.
- Fiona says she has a message to deliver at the Crystal Palace. That is her account, not a narrator-confirmed truth. Sender, recipient, contents and true motive may remain unknown indefinitely. No carried parcel is needed.
- At the palace Alisha gets trapped, but they can still talk. Fiona explores to free her; both contribute through clues and conversation.
- After the rescue, Fiona reaches the recipient’s room and goes inside to deliver the message, leaving Alisha waiting outside. The delivery happens; its contents and Fiona’s full motive need not be revealed.
- Alisha notices a dog she thinks is Cookie and follows it. Fiona comes out, sees her running off and follows. They discover the dog is not Cookie.
- The dog knocks Alisha off balance as it runs past. Several discs spill from her disc golf bag and roll across the floor; one rolls into a floor slot and activates a magical rune gate. It does not merely bump her bag.
- Through the gate Alisha recognizes a disc golf course she has wanted to play. Fiona knows where it is: a hike from Alisha’s village. With her errand finished and time to spare, Fiona agrees to go with her. Opening the gate is accidental; entering together is a believable, voluntary detour. The gate closes behind them.
- Unknown to them, the disc has activated linked rune gates across the land. Their journey home leads into a later missing-friends arc. The exact connection remains open; activation does not automatically abduct people.
- The woodland chapters teach the game while building Alisha and Fiona’s friendship. Five chapters is a working target, not a requirement.
- Keep the old nine boards playable in a collapsed **Previous levels** section, separate from new story progress. Preserve their definitions and tests as reference; make the section removable later.
- Hide the grid while idle. Show subtle placement outlines during selection/dragging, with clear merge/craft targets and keyboard focus. Keep gates and objective markers readable.
- Story presentation follows the Attic: one image and one text beat at a time; tap to advance, Back to reread. Start with text and rough scene descriptions.

## Rough storyboard and text

Proposed dialogue below is deliberately short. Scene descriptions are placeholders, not artwork requests.

### 1 — Meet Fiona

**Opening, rough panels:** Alisha throws along a forest fairway → the disc curves into the trees → she follows → Fiona looks up with the disc caught in her antlers.

> Alisha: “Oh! I’m so sorry.”
>
> Fiona: “Is this yours?”

**Puzzle:** Clear a small route so Alisha can reach Fiona and retrieve the disc; Fiona is not hurt or trapped. A quick familiar merge, then a clearly arranged five-piece group. Teach that five makes two; make its benefit visible without an unexplained failure trap.

**Ending:** Alisha retrieves her disc. They introduce themselves; the conversation leads to walking together.

> Alisha: “Where are you headed?”
>
> Fiona: “The Crystal Palace. Someone there is expecting a message.”
>
> Alisha: “Would you like some company?”
>
> Fiona: “I would.”

### 2 — Woodland discoveries

**Opening:** On the route to the palace, the pair find a small work surface and stored supplies.

> Fiona: “Someone used to come this way.”
>
> Alisha: “Let’s see what they left behind.”

**Puzzle:** Prepare two recipe ingredients, combine them into a named chest, then tap it open. Its fixed reward supplies the final merge. Show the recipe and contents; no surprise resource rules.

**Ending:** Their work opens the way farther along the trail.

> Alisha: “Looks like we can get through now.”
>
> Fiona: “Let’s try it.”

The exact recipe and reward remain to be chosen from existing assets. Do not invent a special map mechanic.

### 3 — The hidden path

**Opening:** A closed woodland gate with an obvious marked stone.

> Fiona: “That mark looks familiar.”
>
> Alisha: “Maybe we can make what it needs.”

**Puzzle:** Two compact areas. Teach placing the exact requested item on a marker; completing it opens the gate, fixes the offering and clears Undo. Clearly introduce that checkpoint before it happens. No cross-room resource transfer.

**Ending:** The gate reveals the palace in the distance.

> Alisha: “Is that it?”
>
> Fiona: “The Crystal Palace.”

### 4 — Crystal Palace

**Opening:** Alisha and Fiona arrive at the palace gate.

> Alisha: “One more locked door.”
>
> Fiona: “We’re getting rather good at those.”

**Puzzle:** Alisha crosses a doorway and a gate closes between them. She can still talk to Fiona. Show Alisha inside the trapped room on the merge board, behind its crystal door, so freeing her is the visible objective. Initially she is a scenery character, not a movable piece or occupied merge cell; Fiona need not be visible. Fiona explores the accessible palace areas; Alisha examines her side and offers clues. Reuse familiar merges and marked objectives, with at most two branches initially. Convey cooperation through dialogue around ordinary puzzles; no new two-character control system is required.

**Ending:** The gate opens and they reunite. Give them a quiet moment of relief before continuing toward the delivery.

> Alisha: “I’m glad you stayed.”
>
> Fiona: “I wasn’t leaving you there.”

### 5 — Beyond the gate

**Opening cutscene:** After the reunion they reach the recipient’s room. Fiona goes in to deliver her spoken message; Alisha waits outside. A familiar-looking dog catches Alisha’s attention.

> Alisha: “Cookie?”

Alisha follows around a corner, then runs after it. Fiona emerges from delivering the message, sees Alisha going and follows. Let recognition and concern explain Alisha’s action; do not make ADHD an explanation or a punchline.

**Puzzle:** A short unfamiliar palace passage, using learned mechanics to reach the dog. Exact board and obstacles remain proposals. No new chase or character-control system is required.

**Gate cutscene, rough panels:**

1. They catch up and realize this is not Cookie. The dog’s identity and whether it is ordinary remain open.
2. Running past, it knocks Alisha off balance. Discs fly from her bag, scatter and roll along the floor.
3. One disc rolls into a floor slot. A click; runes illuminate and a clearly magical gate opens. No secret magical-disc identity is established.
4. Alisha recognizes a disc golf course she has always wanted to play on the far side. Fiona knows the place and the walking route toward Alisha’s village. Her delivery is finished; she has time to accompany Alisha.
5. They choose to cross for a visit to the course, expecting to walk home afterward. This is not a shortcut or a dangerous attempt to retrieve a cheap disc.
6. Once both have crossed, the gate closes behind them. End this arc here: they are together, away from home, with a journey ahead.
7. Optional final montage: the same rune lights up at other old gates across the land. Do not show or explain every consequence.

Allow a reunion and the delivery between the rescue and this incident, rather than cancelling the rescue’s payoff immediately. Exact gate dialogue is still unwritten.

### Following arcs — home and missing friends

- **Journey home:** the course and the route back become the next adventure. Establish friends and familiar places before relying on the emotional weight of their absence. Exact distance, obstacles and chapter count remain open.
- **Homecoming:** Alisha discovers friends are missing. A report of an old gate lighting up, or a matching rune, connects the trouble to the palace activation.
- **Search:** Alisha and Fiona suspect they helped start it and want to find their friends. This is a possible discovery, not knowledge they have when entering the gate.
- **Gate boundary:** opening routes is not automatic abduction. Friends may have crossed; something may have come through. Mechanism, responsibility and eventual explanation remain to be developed.

**Scope:** The intended first arc is the Attic plus five story levels, ending beyond the gate. Journey-home and missing-friends gameplay are future arcs, not part of the first build. Implement and test Meet Fiona first.

## Mystery and tone

Fiona’s behavior should be believable without explaining her whole life. Helping Alisha is a sincere choice even if her errand is not the whole truth. Do not seed a specific secret yet. War, royal identity, sacrifice and illness were playful/exploratory possibilities, not approved plot or tonal commitments. The message is delivered offscreen or privately; no required contents reveal, urgent world-saving mission or explanation for Fiona’s present-day absence.

## Rules and teaching boundaries

- Supply containers hold one or more authored items according to the puzzle’s needs. Named crafted chests keep their specific recipe rewards.
- Container opening releases the batch, clears Undo and removes the container; without space, nothing is released. Retry restores the start.
- Each chapter introduces one main idea. Earlier skills can recur, but do not repeat the full Attic tutorial.
- Explain checkpoint consequences and recipe rewards. Use subtle guidance before adding more dialogue.
- Keep generated modes available for practice and variety; this plan does not change them.

## Decisions to review

1. **Pacing:** Does Attic + five chapters give enough time for the meeting, friendship, rescue, delivery and gate ending? Chapter count may change after testing.
2. **Chapter 2:** Teach a crafted chest here, or keep chest recipes in generated play and shorten the journey?
3. **Puzzle details:** Choose existing recipe rewards, palace gate objectives and the final passage before final dialogue or art.
4. **Gate scene:** Choose the recognizable course landmark, floor-slot staging and how clearly the closing gate changes their plans. Fiona’s knowledge should not imply she expected it to close.
5. **Later arcs:** Decide which friends the player knows, and how the activated gates relate to their absence. The dog, network history and message contents can stay mysterious.
