# Story and training — review draft

**Quick visual review:** [Chapter 1 wireframe storyboard](story/chapter-one-review.html) — proposed cutscenes, puzzle maps and reuse of the original nine levels.

**For review, not an implementation brief.** Start with the playable Attic, then build Chapter 1 from five short playable levels. Reuse existing puzzles where they fit; no new polished artwork yet.

## Direction agreed so far

- Older Alisha tells her granddaughter about her adventures. The granddaughter calls Fiona **Aunt Fiona**. Fiona is absent from the attic; we have not explained why.
- The Attic opens the story: tidy photos, assemble a book, craft the Picture Book, then tap it open.
- First meeting: Alisha is playing disc golf in the forest. Her disc hits a tree. “Stupid tree!” The disc ricochets into Fiona’s antlers; Alisha follows it and meets her. Keep it funny and harmless.
- Fiona says she has a message to deliver at the Crystal Palace. That is her account, not a narrator-confirmed truth. Sender, recipient, contents and true motive may remain unknown indefinitely. No carried parcel is needed.
- At the palace, solving merge objectives reveals a hidden room with a pressure plate. Alisha appears as an interactable character on the board. Her speech bubble says, “Oh, that looks like a pressure plate in that room. I should go stand on it.” The player taps the bubble itself to dismiss it, then drags Alisha onto the highlighted plate. This opens the next room but closes a crystal partition behind her. She stays visible inside; Fiona explores to free her, and they continue talking through character-anchored speech bubbles. The trap happens through the player’s puzzle action, not just in a cutscene.
- After the rescue, Fiona reaches the recipient’s room and goes inside to deliver the message, leaving Alisha waiting outside. The delivery happens; its contents and Fiona’s full motive need not be revealed.
- Alisha notices a dog she thinks is Cookie and follows it. Fiona comes out, sees her running off and follows. They discover the dog is not Cookie.
- The dog knocks Alisha off balance as it runs past. Several discs spill from her disc golf bag and roll across the floor; one rolls into a floor slot and activates a magical rune gate. It does not merely bump her bag.
- Through the gate Alisha recognizes a disc golf course she has wanted to play. Fiona knows where it is: a hike from Alisha’s village. With her errand finished and time to spare, Fiona agrees to go with her. Opening the gate is accidental; entering together is a believable, voluntary detour. The gate closes behind them.
- Unknown to them, the disc has activated linked rune gates across the land. Their journey home leads into a later missing-friends arc. The exact connection remains open; activation does not automatically abduct people.
- The five levels within Chapter 1 teach the game while building Alisha and Fiona’s friendship. Five levels is a working target, not a requirement.
- Keep the old nine boards playable in a collapsed **Previous levels** section, separate from new story progress. Preserve their definitions and tests as reference; make the section removable later.
- Hide the grid while idle. Show subtle placement outlines during selection/dragging, with clear merge/craft targets and keyboard focus. Keep gates and objective markers readable.
- Character placement is a recurring puzzle mechanic: prepare the way with merges, then place Alisha on a marked character spot to act. Teach a safe use before the palace trap. She is a character, never a merge ingredient.
- Story presentation follows the Attic: one image and one text beat at a time; tap to advance, Back to reread. Start with text and rough scene descriptions.

## Rough storyboard and text

Proposed dialogue below is deliberately short. Scene descriptions are placeholders, not artwork requests.

### 1 — Meet Fiona

**Opening:** Alisha throws from a concrete tee pad down a long forest fairway toward a distant basket. The disc strikes a tree and ricochets away into the woods. Alisha says “Stupid tree!” Neither Alisha nor the player sees Fiona yet. Transition into the puzzle as Alisha goes to find her disc.

> Alisha: “Stupid tree!”

**Puzzle:** Work through the woods toward the lost disc. Fiona remains unseen throughout this puzzle; the destination is not the tee or throwing area. A quick familiar merge, then a clearly arranged five-piece group. Teach that five makes two; make its benefit visible without an unexplained failure trap.

**Ending:** After the puzzle, Alisha reaches a secluded glade away from the fairway and discovers her disc resting harmlessly in Fiona’s antlers. Alisha apologizes; Fiona asks, “Is this yours?” Only then does Alisha retrieve it. They introduce themselves, and the conversation leads to walking together.

> Alisha: “Where are you headed?”
>
> Fiona: “The Crystal Palace. Someone there is expecting a message.”
>
> Alisha: “Would you like some company?”
>
> Fiona: “I would.”

### 2 — Woodland discoveries

**Opening:** On the route to the palace, the pair find a small work surface and stored supplies.

> Fiona: “Someone used to come this way.”
>
> Alisha: “Let’s see what they left behind.”

**Puzzle:** A multi-room forest trail with 3–5 clearings (wireframe proposes four). Start in the southern clearing; a short objective opens Songbird clearing. Progress there reveals a hidden fern clearing. Completing objectives in both clearings lights two symbols and opens the north trail exit. Teach exploration, hidden areas and combined room unlocks one at a time. Borrow Hidden Hollow, Hidden Cache and the original Crystal Palace’s recorded tasks.

A fixed-reward crafted chest can remain as familiar practice in one clearing, but is optional until recipe/region compatibility is verified. Keep resources in their own rooms; combine completion records across rooms rather than transferring items.

**Ending:** Their work opens the way farther along the trail.

> Alisha: “Looks like we can get through now.”
>
> Fiona: “Let’s try it.”

The exact recipe and reward remain to be chosen from existing assets. Do not invent a special map mechanic.

### 3 — The palace entrance

**Opening:** Start at the bottom of a larger northbound map. Neither the palace entrance nor its pressure plate is visible yet; they are revealed later through progression.

> Fiona: “That mark looks familiar.”
>
> Alisha: “Maybe we can make what it needs.”

**Puzzle:** A larger 4–6-room approach (wireframe proposes six), progressing bottom to top: starting room → fork → west/east terraces → upper approach → hidden palace forecourt. The fork opens both terraces; their separate completed objectives jointly unlock the upper approach. Its final objective reveals the palace entrance and character plate. Only then can Alisha reach the plate. Item objectives open the route, never the palace entrance itself. Reuse recorded tasks, concealed areas and reveal camera behavior; keep each room’s merge chains manageable. No cross-room resource transfer.

**Safe character introduction:** Once the approach is clear, highlight Alisha and the now-accessible plate beside the entrance. She offers to stand there; dismiss her speech bubble by tapping the bubble itself, then drag her onto the plate (or select her and activate the spot). Only this placement opens the palace entrance. No trap: this is the safe first use of the same interaction that later traps her inside. Keep this short and show the entrance opening on the board.

**Ending:** Their work unlocks the palace entrance. A short cutscene shows them going inside.

> Alisha: “We’re in.”
>
> Fiona: “The Crystal Palace.”

### 4 — Crystal Palace

**Opening:** Alisha and Fiona enter the palace. Their route toward the message recipient is blocked farther inside.

> Alisha: “One more locked door.”
>
> Fiona: “We’re getting rather good at those.”

**Puzzle progression:**

1. Completing merge objectives reveals a hidden side room containing a pressure plate linked to the next door.
2. A speech bubble anchored to Alisha appears on the board:

   > Alisha: “Oh, that looks like a pressure plate in that room. I should go stand on it.”

3. The bubble stays until the player taps **the bubble itself**, not elsewhere on the screen. Include a small “Tap to continue” hint. Pause board interactions while it is open; the dismissing tap must not also move anything. Support focused Enter/Space dismissal. Keep navigation available.
4. After dismissal, highlight Alisha and the plate. The player drags Alisha onto it. This opens the next room but also closes a crystal partition behind her, trapping her visibly on the merge board.
5. Alisha and Fiona can still see/hear each other. Fiona explores the newly accessible palace areas; Alisha observes her side and offers clues through further deliberately dismissed speech bubbles. Fiona need not appear on the board.
6. Familiar merges and marked objectives release Alisha. Freeing her is the level’s visible goal, rather than collecting another object.

**Character scope:** Reuse the marked-spot interaction introduced safely at the palace entrance. Alisha is interactable for the plate placement, replacing the earlier scenery-only proposal. Keep her separate from mergeable items and ordinary item cells. While trapped she cannot be dragged out; releasing the partition restores her freedom through the level event. Fiona need not become a second controllable character.

**Trap presentation:** Show the newly opened door and the closing partition on the board. Proposed reaction:

> Alisha: “…Fiona?”
>
> Fiona: “I’m here.”

**Checkpoint proposal:** Placement commits the gate event and clears Undo; Retry restores the complete level. Teach the checkpoint rule beforehand without spoiling the surprise. At most two rescue branches initially; no full two-character gameplay system.

**Ending:** The gate opens and they reunite. Give them a quiet moment of relief before continuing toward the delivery.

> Alisha: “I’m glad you stayed.”
>
> Fiona: “I wasn’t leaving you there.”

### 5 — Beyond the gate

**Opening cutscene:** After the reunion they reach the recipient’s room. Fiona goes in to deliver her spoken message; Alisha waits outside. A familiar-looking dog catches Alisha’s attention.

> Alisha: “Cookie?”

Alisha follows around a corner, then runs after it. Fiona emerges from delivering the message, sees Alisha going and follows. Let recognition and concern explain Alisha’s action; do not make ADHD an explanation or a punchline.

**Puzzle:** A short unfamiliar palace passage, using learned mechanics to reach the dog. Exact board and obstacles remain proposals. No new chase or character-control system is required.

**Gate cutscene, rough panels:**

1. They catch up and realize this is not Cookie. The dog’s identity and whether it is ordinary remain open.
2. Running past, it knocks Alisha off balance. Discs fly from her bag, scatter and roll along the floor.
3. One disc rolls into a floor slot. A click; runes illuminate and a clearly magical gate opens. No secret magical-disc identity is established.
4. Alisha recognizes a disc golf course she has always wanted to play on the far side. Fiona knows the place and the walking route toward Alisha’s village. Her delivery is finished; she has time to accompany Alisha.
5. They choose to cross for a visit to the course, expecting to walk home afterward. This is not a shortcut or a dangerous attempt to retrieve a cheap disc.
6. Once both have crossed, the gate closes behind them. End this arc here: they are together, away from home, with a journey ahead.
7. Optional final montage: the same rune lights up at other old gates across the land. Do not show or explain every consequence.

Allow a reunion and the delivery between the rescue and this incident, rather than cancelling the rescue’s payoff immediately. Exact gate dialogue is still unwritten.

### Following arcs — home and missing friends

- **Journey home:** the course and the route back become the next adventure. Establish friends and familiar places before relying on the emotional weight of their absence. Exact distance, obstacles and chapter count remain open.
- **Homecoming:** Alisha discovers friends are missing. A report of an old gate lighting up, or a matching rune, connects the trouble to the palace activation.
- **Search:** Alisha and Fiona suspect they helped start it and want to find their friends. This is a possible discovery, not knowledge they have when entering the gate.
- **Gate boundary:** opening routes is not automatic abduction. Friends may have crossed; something may have come through. Mechanism, responsibility and eventual explanation remain to be developed.

**Scope:** The intended first arc is the Attic prologue plus Chapter 1’s five story levels, ending beyond the gate. Journey-home and missing-friends gameplay are future arcs, not part of the first build. Implement and test Meet Fiona first.

## Mystery and tone

Fiona’s behavior should be believable without explaining her whole life. Helping Alisha is a sincere choice even if her errand is not the whole truth. Do not seed a specific secret yet. War, royal identity, sacrifice and illness were playful/exploratory possibilities, not approved plot or tonal commitments. The message is delivered offscreen or privately; no required contents reveal, urgent world-saving mission or explanation for Fiona’s present-day absence.

## Rules and teaching boundaries

- **Character spots:** drag Alisha onto clearly marked character destinations; also support select-then-tap and keyboard activation. Highlight valid spots when she is selected. Ordinary items cannot activate them, and Alisha cannot merge, swap with items or be dropped on arbitrary item cells.
- **First implementation:** one available character destination at a time. No free walking, pathfinding, inventory carried by Alisha or character swapping. Position and completed actions must restore correctly on Retry; exact Undo/checkpoint behavior is specified per action rather than every placement automatically clearing Undo.
- **Access rules to settle in the prototype:** a spot must be in a revealed, accessible area. Character placement must not jump across locked doors or let Alisha escape the palace trap. Character spots use their own visual markers even while the ordinary grid is hidden.
- **Later uses, not first-build requirements:** holding a door open, examining a rune for a clue, using a workbench to enable crafting, or standing beside a container to open it. Start with the pressure plate; do not require Alisha for every existing interaction or retroactively change generated modes.
- Supply containers hold one or more authored items according to the puzzle’s needs. Named crafted chests keep their specific recipe rewards.
- Container opening releases the batch, clears Undo and removes the container; without space, nothing is released. Retry restores the start.
- Each level introduces one main idea. Earlier skills can recur, but do not repeat the full Attic tutorial.
- Explain checkpoint consequences and recipe rewards. Use subtle guidance before adding more dialogue.
- Keep generated modes available for practice and variety; this plan does not change them.

## Decisions to review

1. **Pacing:** Does Attic + five levels give enough time for the meeting, friendship, rescue, delivery and gate ending? Level count may change after testing.
2. **Forest trail:** Confirm the proposed four clearings within the 3–5 range, and whether familiar chest crafting fits alongside exploration/reveal lessons.
3. **Palace approach:** Confirm six rooms within the 4–6 range and the two-branch unlock. Exact room inventories, reveal timing and final map dimensions need validation.
4. **Gate scene:** Choose the recognizable course landmark, floor-slot staging and how clearly the closing gate changes their plans. Fiona’s knowledge should not imply she expected it to close.
5. **Later arcs:** Decide which friends the player knows, and how the activated gates relate to their absence. The dog, network history and message contents can stay mysterious.
6. **Character prototype:** Confirm the marked-spot presentation, accessible-area restriction and safe entrance demonstration. Decide whether the first plate holds a door open only while occupied or records a completed action; the palace trap records a checkpoint. Avoid adding both behaviors until teaching needs them.
